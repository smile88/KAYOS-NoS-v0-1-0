# Nano Banana 2 — Umbraveil City Map
### Asset: **EN-018 · "The Umbraveil Survey"** (add to Asset Bible, Environment, P3)
**Concept:** an in-world Noctari cartographic artifact — the capital drawn as a *light survey*, because in Umbraveil the only thing worth mapping is where the sun lands and where it goes afterward.
**Deliver:** 2:3 portrait, high resolution. NOT pixel art.
**Companion piece to:** EN-017 "The Starfall Plate." Designed to hang next to it and lose an argument.

---

## THE DESIGN PROBLEM (read this first — it's why the prompt looks like it does)

Starfall is a **ring**; Umbraveil is a **ribbon**. Starfall is a city that engineered *darkness*; Umbraveil is a city that harvests *light*. Canon gives us the hinge: Umbraveil is the Noctari capital, built in a mountain valley receiving **only two hours of direct sunlight daily**, and Noctari crystal arrays exist to **capture and enhance ambient light rather than block it**.

So: Starfall's arrays run in reverse, throwing light away. **Umbraveil's run forward.** For two hours a day the sun crosses the slot of the valley overhead, the light-farms on the upper walls catch it, and the city lives on that harvest for the other twenty-two. Light is stored, carried, and distributed — the **lantern rivers** of the asset bible are literal: light flows downhill through the city like water.

Three consequences, and they generate the whole map:

1. **Down is up.** Noctari find direct sun harsh. So the deep, permanently-shaded valley floor is the *most* desirable address — the capital's seat, the observation houses. The upper terraces, closest to the two-hour sun, are where the light-farms are and where the people who work them live. Umbraveil's poor are on the skyline, in the light, visible to everyone. *The exact inversion of Starfall, where they're underneath.*
2. **The over-open doors** (canon, from the outline's Ch1). If light is harvested and shared, hoarding it is the civic sin. Doors stay open so light flows between homes. Elorin grew up in a city where nothing was ever closed — and became a woman who buried a secret flaw inside the most dangerous object in the world. **The two cities are her arc.** Starfall shutters its windows by law; Umbraveil never closes a door.
3. **The map is of a moment, not a place.** A survey of Umbraveil that doesn't show the sun is useless. So the plate has the two-hour sun *drawn on it*.

## THE PROMPT (copy the whole block)

A highly detailed fantasy city map rendered as an antique engineering light-survey, in a 2:3 vertical portrait composition. Precise, scholarly, engraved line work.

**Subject:** Umbraveil, the night-elf capital, built inside a deep, narrow mountain valley that receives only two hours of direct sunlight each day. The valley runs vertically down the length of the plate, top to bottom — a long ribbon of city between two enormous steep walls.

**Style:** the aesthetic of a 19th-century surveyor's plate crossed with a celestial atlas. Silver-white engraved line work on a deep twilight blue-violet ground. Dense contour hatching for the valley walls. Fine, obsessive technical precision — but warmer and older than a star chart, with the softened edges and faint foxing of a plate that has been folded and refolded for centuries.

**Structure — draw all of these:**

1. **The valley walls:** two vast masses of contour hatching running down the left and right of the plate, squeezing the city into a narrow ribbon between them. The walls should feel oppressive and enormous — the city is at the bottom of a slot.
2. **The shadow contours (the plate's organising system):** instead of elevation lines, the map is banded by **duration of sunlight** — irregular contour bands sweeping across the valley, each marking how many minutes of direct sun that ground receives. Bands are widest and brightest near the top of the walls and collapse to nothing on the valley floor. This is the map's spine: it is a topography of shade.
3. **The two-hour sun:** a single hard-edged wedge of pale gold light lies diagonally across the plate, falling down the slot of the valley and striking the upper western wall — the actual noon sunbeam, drawn as a geometric solid, with its angle annotated. **This is the plate's central conceit: a city map with a time in it.** Everything else on the map is arranged in relation to where this wedge lands.
4. **The light-farms:** on the upper walls where the wedge strikes — terraced ranks of angled crystal arrays, drawn like vineyard terraces, each blade angled to catch the beam. Rendered in fine geometric detail. Small, cramped dwellings packed between the terraces.
5. **The lantern rivers:** from the light-farms, **channels of warm amber-gold** run down the valley walls and thread through the city, branching and rejoining like a river delta, glowing faintly against the cold blue plate. They are the only warm thing on the map. Draw them as the map's circulatory system — the most alive element on an otherwise austere document.
6. **The terraced city:** stepped dwellings cut into both valley walls, descending toward the floor, becoming grander, larger and more widely spaced the lower they go. Draw the buildings **with no doors** — open archways and pass-throughs everywhere, light channels running straight through the interiors of houses.
7. **The valley floor:** the deepest, darkest, most spacious district — the capital's seat. Great halls cut into the cliff bases, and several **domed observatory towers** in the deep shade, each marked with a small heraldic sigil in antique gold. The most desirable ground in the city is the ground the sun has never touched.
8. **The river:** an actual water river running the length of the valley floor, drawn in cold silver, parallel to and crossed by the amber light-channels.
9. **The lower gate:** at the bottom of the plate, where the valley narrows to a throat — a great deep gate leading into the mountain, annotated as the road to the Terran territories.
10. **The cross-section (along the right margin):** a tall, narrow technical profile diagram of the valley cut crosswise — both walls in silhouette, the slot of sky between them, the sun's arc drawn as a dotted path with only a short bright segment where it crosses the gap, and light-ray lines striking the farm terraces at annotated angles. A working engineer's diagram, drawn with the same care as the map itself.

**Marginalia:** light-yield tables in neat columns; small angle and reflection diagrams; a scale bar; ornamental filigree corners in antique gold; fold-creases and faint age-staining across the whole plate.

**Palette (strict):** deep twilight blue-violet ground; silver-white line work; **pale gold for the sunbeam wedge**; **warm amber for the light channels only**; antique gold for sigils and filigree. No other colours. The warmth must feel rationed.

**Mood:** a beautiful, careful document about scarcity. The tenderness of people who measure light because they do not have enough of it.

**Critical:** clean engraved linework, no anti-aliased glow, no lens flare, no modern typography. The amber channels glow by colour and value only, not by bloom effects. Do not add a border frame beyond the corner filigree.

## PRACTICAL NOTES

**1. Text will be gibberish — same fix as Starfall.** Add `no text, no lettering, no labels, no numerals — leave label areas blank` and set real type in Affinity's Vector studio. The margin tables and the section diagram's annotations are exactly where you'll want crisp, real type.

**2. The wedge is the shot.** If the sunbeam comes out as a soft glow, a lens flare, or ambient warm lighting, regenerate. It must read as a **hard-edged geometric solid** — a surveyed object, not an atmospheric effect. That hardness is the whole point: these people have measured the sun to the minute because they have to.

**3. Watch for the model flattening the valley.** Models want to make valleys wide and pretty. If that happens, add: `extremely narrow deep slot canyon, sheer vertical walls dominating the composition, city compressed into a thin ribbon, claustrophobic`.

**4. Keep it in the same plate family as EN-017.** Same engraved register, same filigree, same paper logic — so the two hang together as products of one civilisation. The *differences* (ring vs ribbon, cold vs rationed-warm, a hole showing sky vs a beam lying across) should read as two cities' characters, not two art styles.

## BONUS VARIANT — and here this one matters more than Starfall's

Umbraveil appears in the game **only in memory scenes** (EN-009 — "dreamlike softness"). Elorin left as a young woman and never really returned. So a pin-sharp survey plate is, arguably, the *wrong* artifact for how the player will ever experience the place.

Generate the emotionally true one:

> A child's map of a home city, drawn in soft coloured chalks on dark grey paper, kept and refolded for nine hundred years. Deep blue-violet ground, the linework unsteady and confident in the way children are confident. The proportions are all wrong: one house is enormous and drawn in loving detail, the great halls of the capital are a scribble in the corner, and the light-channels are fat glowing amber ribbons three times too wide because they were the best thing. The two-hour sun is drawn as a simple gold band across the top with a small figure standing in it, arms out. Much of the valley is simply absent — not shaded, just never drawn, because she never went there. Soft edges, chalk dust, one corner worn to a rounded nub from handling. Warm, imprecise, and completely certain about what mattered.

**Why this is the better asset of the two:** the survey plate is the city that exists. The chalk map is the city Elorin carries — and the city she carries doesn't exist, and hasn't for nine centuries, and she knows it. Put them side by side and you have her entire interior life without a line of dialogue. Use the chalk map as the transition image into every Umbraveil memory scene.

*(Also: give her the chalk map as an inventory keepsake — IT-003's neighbour. A vision trigger. And in the Coda, Talindir either has it or he doesn't, depending on `P1_TALINDIR_TRUTH`.)*

## CANON NOTES — what's locked vs. what I've built

**From canon (compendium + your outline + asset bible):** Umbraveil is the Noctari capital, in a mountain valley with two hours of direct sun daily · Noctari architecture is stone and crystal, designed to capture and enhance ambient light · elaborate crystal arrays redirect and filter light · society is structured around observation houses that are simultaneously research centres and government · close Terran relations (hence the lower gate) · Elorin is from here, low-born · Seravin Hollow-Water is here · "the over-open doors" and "the void as presence, not absence" (outline Ch1) · "terraced shadow-gardens, deep-blue twilight, lantern rivers" (asset bible EN-009).

**New here — my calls, flag if you disagree:** the light-harvest economy and the two-hour beam as a civic event · light as a stored, flowed, rationable commodity ("lantern rivers" made literal) · **down = up** (the shaded valley floor as the noble district, the sunlit upper walls as the workers' ground) · the over-open doors as an anti-hoarding custom rather than just an architectural quirk · the lower gate to Terran roads · Umbraveil's official map being a light-survey rather than a street map.

**Worth a ruling:** if the light-farm workers live high in the harsh sun, and Elorin is *low-born* — is "low-born" literal here? Because in this city, low is noble. Two readings: (a) "low-born" is generic and she's from the upper terraces, meaning **the girl who grew up in the only Noctari place that got sunlight dedicated her life to the void** — which is almost too good; or (b) the phrase means what it usually means and Umbraveil's status axis works some other way. I lean hard toward (a). Your call.
